The current that flows in a capacitor during charging
(I = CdV/dt) has some unusual features. Unlike resistive
current, it’s not proportional to voltage, but rather to the
rate of change (the “time derivative”) of voltage. Furthermore, unlike the situation in a resistor, the power (V×I) associated with capacitive current is not turned into heat, but
is stored as energy in the capacitor’s internal electric field.
You get all that energy back when you discharge the capacitor. We’ll see another way to look at these curious properties when we talk about reactance, beginning in §1.7.
